==============================================================================
 UniMic - Step by step setup
==============================================================================

 UniMic turns your phone into a microphone for your computer.
 There is no app to install on the phone. You just open a web page on it.

 Find the section for your computer below and follow it in order.
 Each one takes about five minutes the first time.


==============================================================================
 WINDOWS
==============================================================================

 STEP 1. Unzip the download
 --------------------------
   Right-click the UniMic zip file and choose "Extract All...".
   Pick somewhere easy to find, like your Desktop.
   Open the folder it creates.

 STEP 2. Start it
 ----------------
   Double-click:   unimic.bat

   A black window opens and checks two things:

     - Python. If you do not have it, it offers to install it. Say yes.
     - A virtual audio cable. If you do not have one, it offers to install
       VB-CABLE. Say yes, and approve the Windows admin prompt that appears.

   If it installs either one, close the window and double-click unimic.bat
   again when it finishes.

 STEP 3. Allow it through the firewall
 -------------------------------------
   The first time, Windows asks whether to let Python accept connections.
   Tick "Private networks" and click Allow.

   This prompt only appears once. If you click Cancel by mistake, your phone
   will not be able to load the page and nothing will tell you why. See
   FIXING THINGS at the bottom.

 STEP 4. Open the address on your phone
 --------------------------------------
   The window shows something like:

       Open this on your phone:   https://192.168.1.190:8443/

   Type that into your phone's browser. Both devices must be on the same
   Wi-Fi network.

 STEP 5. Get past the certificate warning
 ----------------------------------------
   Your phone will warn that the connection is not private. This is normal
   and it is explained at the end of this file.

   Tap "Advanced", then "Proceed".

 STEP 6. Start the microphone
 ----------------------------
   Tap the big "Start microphone" button, and allow microphone access when
   the browser asks.

 STEP 7. Choose it in your app
 -----------------------------
   In Discord, Zoom, OBS or whatever you are using, open the audio settings
   and pick this as the input device:

       CABLE Output

   Done. Talk into your phone.


==============================================================================
 macOS
==============================================================================

 STEP 1. Unzip the download
 --------------------------
   Double-click the UniMic zip file. A folder appears next to it.
   Open that folder.

 STEP 2. Start it
 ----------------
   Double-click:   unimic.command

   >>> THE FIRST TIME, MACOS WILL PROBABLY REFUSE TO OPEN IT. <<<
   >>> THIS IS NORMAL. STEP 3 FIXES IT PERMANENTLY.           <<<

   You will see a message saying the file is from an unidentified developer,
   or that it was blocked to protect your Mac.

 STEP 3. Allow it to run (first time only)
 -----------------------------------------
   You must try to open it once before macOS will offer you the button, so
   do not skip Step 2.

   1. Open System Settings (the grey gear icon).
   2. Click "Privacy & Security" in the left sidebar.
   3. Scroll all the way down to the "Security" section.
   4. You will see a line about unimic.command being blocked, with an
      "Open Anyway" button next to it. Click it.
   5. Enter your password or use Touch ID if asked.
   6. Double-click unimic.command again. Click "Open" on the confirmation.

   It will now open normally every time from here on.

   If you cannot find the button, there are two other ways:

     - Control-click (or right-click) unimic.command, choose "Open" from
       the menu, then click "Open" in the box that appears.

     - Or skip the launcher completely. Open the Terminal app, type
       "cd " (with the space), drag the UniMic folder onto the Terminal
       window, press Return, then type this and press Return:

           python3 unimic.py

       This always works and macOS never blocks it.

 STEP 4. Let it set itself up
 ----------------------------
   The window checks two things:

     - Python. If you do not have it, it offers to install it. Say yes.
     - BlackHole, a free audio driver that lets apps hear the phone. If you
       do not have it, it offers to install it. Say yes and enter your
       password when asked.

   If it installs BlackHole, restart your Mac, or log out and back in.
   macOS will not see the new audio device until you do. Then double-click
   unimic.command again.

 STEP 5. Open the address on your phone
 --------------------------------------
   The window shows something like:

       Open this on your phone:   https://192.168.1.190:8443/

   Type that into your phone's browser. Both devices must be on the same
   Wi-Fi network.

 STEP 6. Get past the certificate warning
 ----------------------------------------
   Your phone will warn that the connection is not private. This is normal
   and it is explained at the end of this file.

   Tap "Advanced", then "Proceed".

 STEP 7. Start the microphone
 ----------------------------
   Tap the big "Start microphone" button, and allow microphone access when
   the browser asks.

 STEP 8. Choose it in your app
 -----------------------------
   In Zoom, Discord, QuickTime or whatever you are using, open the audio
   settings and pick this as the input device:

       BlackHole 2ch

   Done. Talk into your phone.


==============================================================================
 LINUX
==============================================================================

 There is nothing to install. UniMic creates the microphone itself.

 STEP 1. Unzip the download
 --------------------------
   Unzip it wherever you like, then open a terminal in that folder.

 STEP 2. Start it
 ----------------
       python3 unimic.py

   If it complains that pactl is missing, install your distribution's
   PulseAudio utilities package. On Debian and Ubuntu:

       sudo apt install pulseaudio-utils

 STEP 3. Open the address on your phone
 --------------------------------------
   It prints something like:

       Open this on your phone:   https://192.168.1.190:8443/

   Type that into your phone's browser. Both devices must be on the same
   network.

 STEP 4. Get past the certificate warning
 ----------------------------------------
   Tap "Advanced", then "Proceed".

 STEP 5. Start the microphone
 ----------------------------
   Tap "Start microphone" and allow microphone access.

 STEP 6. Choose it in your app
 -----------------------------
   Pick this as the input device:

       UniMic (Phone)

   Done. Talk into your phone.


==============================================================================
 WHY DOES MY PHONE SAY THE CONNECTION IS NOT PRIVATE?
==============================================================================

 Browsers refuse to hand over the microphone unless the page is served over
 HTTPS. HTTPS needs a certificate, and certificates are normally issued for
 real domain names on the internet. Your computer at home does not have one,
 so UniMic makes its own.

 Your phone has no way to check a home-made certificate, so it warns you.
 That warning is your phone doing its job.

 It is safe to tap through here, and it is worth knowing why: the warning
 means "I cannot prove who this is", not "this is broken". You are typing
 your own computer's address, on your own network. Tapping Proceed once tells
 the phone to remember it.

 The encryption itself is real either way. Without it, anyone else on your
 Wi-Fi could listen to your microphone.


==============================================================================
 FIXING THINGS
==============================================================================

 Is my computer even ready?
   Run this. It checks everything and tells you what is missing:

       python3 unimic.py --check

 The phone cannot load the page
   Both devices must be on the same Wi-Fi. Phones on mobile data cannot
   reach your computer.

   On Windows this is almost always the firewall. Open Windows Defender
   Firewall, find Python in the list of allowed apps, and tick the
   "Private" box.

 Audio stops when the phone screen turns off
   Leave "Keep screen awake" switched on in the page.

 The app does not list the microphone
   Some apps only look for microphones when they start. Close the app
   completely and open it again with UniMic already running.

 It sounds quiet, or crackly
   Use the "Mic volume" slider on the phone page. If the meter turns amber
   and shows CLIP, it is too loud. Slide it back down.

 Choppy or robotic audio
   Wi-Fi trouble. Move closer to the router, or turn on your phone's
   hotspot and connect the computer to that instead.

 Stopping it
   Press Ctrl and C together in the UniMic window.


==============================================================================
 UPDATING
==============================================================================

 You do not need to do anything. When a new version comes out, UniMic asks
 you the next time you start it:

     UniMic v1.1.0 is available. This is 1.0.1.
     Install it now? [Y/n]

 Press Enter to say yes. It installs the new version and restarts itself.
 Press n if you would rather not. It will ask again another day.

 It never installs anything without asking first. Your certificates and
 anything else you put in the folder are not touched.

 The version you are running is shown in the box every time it starts, on
 the same line as the UniMic name.

 If you prefer to do it by hand:

     python3 unimic.py --version    shows what you have
     python3 unimic.py --update     installs the newest version

 Full documentation is in README.md.
